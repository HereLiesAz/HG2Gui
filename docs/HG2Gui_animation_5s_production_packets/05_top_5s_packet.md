# Animation 05 — `top` — process monitor

## Chapter 7 — Processes

## Source animation sequence

A leaderboard appears beneath:

`SORT: %CPU`

1. sensible_process — 2%
2. useful_process — 1%
3. idle_process — 0%

A clipboard marked **PERFORMANCE REVIEW** enters frame.

The process at the bottom starts eating CPU.

Its percentage rises.

The others notice the clipboard.

Soon every process is shovelling CPU into itself while climbing the board.

One reaches 99%.

A little trophy appears.

The terminal freezes.

The Board records a record quarter.

## Sequence lock

**Guide tabs:** `TOP` / `PERFORMANCE`

**Palette:** mustard, dark teal, mint green, vermilion, dusty lavender

**Total duration:** 15 seconds (3 × 5-second cuts)

## Shared visual prompt

Create one EXACTLY 5.0-second segment of an original animated Guide entry that can sit visually beside the Hitchhiker's Guide graphics in the 2005 film. Continue the visual grammar; do not reproduce an existing film shot, character design, or composition.

REFERENCE LANGUAGE
- Use the supplied movie screenshots as visual anchors for proportions, graphic density, palette behavior, and motion-graphic restraint.
- Active composition is cinematic 2.35:1. If the generator only outputs 16:9, place a centered 2.35:1 active picture inside the 16:9 frame with clean black letterboxing; never stretch the artwork.
- Flat 2D vector / cut-paper / screen-printed pictogram look, closer to an international information sign than a modern sci-fi HUD.
- Persistent warm mustard Guide header across the top of the active picture. At upper left, two connected capsule tabs extend from / clip against the left edge. The first tab is the subject, the second the subtopic. Use the exact tab labels supplied below.
- Typography: Jost ExtraBold/800 or a near geometric equivalent, uppercase, generous tracking. Text is a designed graphic element, not subtitles.
- Use only 4–7 matte colors in the scene. No gradients. No gloss. No bloom. No drop shadows. No translucent glass. No depth-of-field. Very light imperfect print/screen texture is welcome; edges may feel slightly handmade.
- People, aliens, machines, files and processes are simplified geometric silhouettes and cutout shapes with deliberately economical anatomy. Minimal facial detail. Express ideas through pose, scale, labels and objects rather than acting or lip-sync.
- Favor bold asymmetric composition and large areas of empty color. Perspective is flattened. Diagrams may become rooms; labels may become physical objects; metaphors are treated as literal information graphics.
- Motion is crisp, graphic and primarily linear: slides, pivots, hinges, flips, rotations, wipes, mechanical relabeling, 2D pans and zooms. No camera shake. No photorealistic physics. No dissolves or soft fades. Avoid bouncy modern easing; changes should feel diagrammatic and inevitable.
- Comedy must be dry. Nobody performs the joke for camera. The visual system explains a technically correct thing, follows one innocent word too literally, and lets the consequence become absurd.
- No generic outer-space imagery unless the beat specifically requires it. No neon cyberpunk, holograms, glossy app UI, anime, 3D cartoon rendering, Pixar-like character animation, cinematic lens effects, or realistic terminal windows.
- Do not invent extra dialogue or labels. For text longer than a few words, reserve a clean sign/card area and composite the exact typography in post if the video model cannot render it reliably.
