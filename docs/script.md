# THE CARTOGRAPHICAL DISASTER

**Placement:** immediately before the canonical `find` sequence  
**Duration:** 30 seconds — 6 × 5-second cuts

The command `find` is named after the Cartographical Disaster of Nth.

Before the Disaster, finding something required a map showing every legitimate route by which it might be reached.

This sounded comprehensive.

Comprehensiveness required detours.

Detours required signs.

Signs required committees to decide which direction counted as forward.

Eventually maps began including the preferences of travellers who had not yet decided whether they were travelling.

The number of routes exceeded the number of destinations, followed shortly by the number of dimensions available to draw them in.

The Cartographers solved the problem by abolishing routes.

Their replacement system asked where to begin and what, exactly, one was looking for.

Unix retained the useful part.

`find` starts at one or more paths, recursively examines entries beneath them, and applies tests such as name, type, or modification time.

The Cartographers regard this as cheating and have been unable to locate the appeals office.
