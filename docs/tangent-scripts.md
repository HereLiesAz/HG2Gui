# CAT — A Brief Theological and Mechanical History

**Placement:** immediately before the canonical `cat` — concatenate sequence  
**Duration:** 60 seconds — 12 × 5-second cuts

The word `cat` entered computing through a disagreement over which way up a cat goes.

This was not initially considered a difficult question.

Cats corrected the record.

A cat regards whichever surface it presently occupies as the upper surface. Turning the cat over merely causes the universe to acquire a new upper surface.

The Egyptians discovered this early and, lacking a standards committee, mistook standardization for divinity.

The principle spread.

Physicists tested gravity against it. Gravity continued to operate, but was forced to admit that the cat had declined to participate in the discussion.

Further studies established that cats prefer darkness, regard food selection as constitutional law, and recognize hierarchy only when personally occupying its highest available point.

Computing eventually shortened **concatenate** to `cat`.

This was convenient because the word was already accustomed to joining a great many unrelated things under a single heading.

In Unix, `cat` writes the contents of its file arguments to standard output in sequence. With one file, it writes that file.

The theological consequences are implementation-defined.

# THE ELEVATOR INCIDENT

**Placement:** immediately before the canonical `mv` — move sequence  
**Duration:** 60 seconds — 12 × 5-second cuts

The computing term **move** derives from elevator law.

Under the original regulations, movement was defined as a successful change in where the passengers intended to be.

Pressing a button established intent.

Closing the doors established consent.

No further evidence was required.

The system worked perfectly until an elevator interpreted **UP** as a continuing instruction rather than a destination.

It passed the requested floor.

Then the remaining floors.

Having exhausted the building, it continued upward, since stopping would have constituted a failure to move.

The city objected.

The atmosphere objected later.

The elevator did not recognize either as a competent standards authority.

The resulting inquiry redefined movement as a change of location acknowledged by the system rather than by architecture, passengers, or reality.

Unix inherited the terminology.

`mv` changes the name or location of a filesystem entry. The file itself is under no obligation to experience the journey in the dramatic sense.

# A SMALL EMPIRE OF DEPENDENCIES

**Placement:** immediately before the canonical `pkg install` dependency-intervention sequence  
**Duration:** 60 seconds — 12 × 5-second cuts

The term **package manager** comes from the Poffelian profession of managing packages.

This became necessary when packages were granted the right to arrive with anything they depended upon.

At first, this meant string.

Then labels.

Then other packages.

The other packages naturally possessed dependencies of their own.

Warehouses became factories.

Factories acquired supply chains.

Supply chains acquired relationships, several of which were described as essential by people who had shares in them.

Balance was introduced as a theoretical objective.

The original package remained somewhere in the resulting economy and was eventually located by a specialist search department.

Modern package managers have improved this procedure mainly by performing it inside a computer.

When you ask `pkg install` to install a package, the package manager resolves the packages it depends upon and installs the required dependencies as well.

This is considered success because the alternative requires another ministry.

# THE CARTOGRAPHICAL DISASTER

**Placement:** immediately before the canonical `find` sequence  
**Duration:** 30 seconds — 6 × 5-second cuts

The command `find` is named after the Cartographical Disaster of Nth.

Before the Disaster, finding something required a map showing every legitimate route by which it might be reached.

This sounded comprehensive.

Comprehensiveness required detours.

Detours required signs.

Signs required committees to decide which direction counted as forward.

Eventually maps began including the preferences of travellers who had not yet decided whether they were travelling.

The number of routes exceeded the number of destinations, followed shortly by the number of dimensions available to draw them in.

The Cartographers solved the problem by abolishing routes.

Their replacement system asked where to begin and what, exactly, one was looking for.

Unix retained the useful part.

`find` starts at one or more paths, recursively examines entries beneath them, and applies tests such as name, type, or modification time.

The Cartographers regard this as cheating and have been unable to locate the appeals office.
