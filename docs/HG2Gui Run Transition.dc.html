HG2Gui — running a command
The pill becomes the page

Tap the last choice and it does not hand off to a new screen — it grows. The pill runs out around the edge of the display, closes the loop, then floods the space it has enclosed. Output is set on that flood, so a command's result carries the colour of the command that made it.

Tap status in the frame. Tap Back and the whole thing runs in reverse, back into the pill it came from.

The six beats
300ms Stretch. The chosen pill gets a little longer to the right, then snaps in from the left down to less than half its width — and flies right at the exact speed that snap was retracting at, one continuous motion into the far edge.
140ms Break free. On impact the pill rocks rapidly on its right tip — the far edge it just flew into — damping out, then falls straight down to the bottom edge. The rest of the stack sweeps off to the left as it goes.
300ms Fall. Free of the stack it drops straight down, from exactly where it broke, and lands on the bottom edge.
840ms Run the perimeter. It stretches along the bottom, then up the right edge, back across the top, down the left — counter-clockwise, one edge at a time, until the loop closes on itself.
420ms Flood. The colour swipes straight up from the bottom edge until the enclosed space is full. No fade — it rises like a tide.
90ms Set. Output wipes on in reading order, one line at a time, exactly as it does on the page — only now the ground is the command's own hue.

The perimeter run exists at two fidelities. The full version above animates four legs in sequence and is used wherever the transition anchors to a specific pill — a trail crumb the animation has to start from. A simplified version interpolates one rectangle with a border and is correct for an ordinary screen open. A simplified reveal is not a broken one.

Going back is the same four beats played backwards, so the result folds down into the pill that produced it. Nothing is ever pushed on or off from somewhere unseen.
